Flag (lift-off) hinges

A flag hinge has two separate leaves. One carries a fixed pin and the other has a hollow knuckle that drops over it, so you can lift the door straight off without knocking out a pin. That makes them popular on machinery and electrical panels, equipment covers, removable gates and metal doors. They come in two mounting styles: screw-on leaves for wood or sheet metal, and weld-on versions for steel frames.

Single vs double crank

"Crank" means the leaf is bent. On a single (half) cranked hinge, one leaf is bent 90 degrees and the other stays flat. On a double (full) cranked hinge, both leaves are bent, which lets the door swing much further open, often all the way back against the wall. Pick the crank to match how your door sits in its frame.

Left hand or right hand

Flag hinges are handed: a left-hand hinge won't work on a right-hand door. A common rule is to push the door open from the outside; if the hinges are on your right, you need right-hand hinges. Brands don't all define handing the same way, so check the seller's diagram before ordering.

Pivot hinges

A pivot hinge doesn't hang the door from its edge like a butt hinge. Instead, the door turns on pivots at the top and bottom, and its weight goes into the floor and the head of the frame. That's why pivots handle heavy and oversized doors well, and why they're used for double-acting doors, storefronts and hidden "bookcase" doors.

  • Center-hung pivots sit within the door's thickness. They're nearly invisible and are often used on double-acting doors that swing both ways.
  • Offset pivots sit near the door's edge so the door swings clear of the frame. They're usually handed and used on single-acting doors.

Pipe hinges

Pipe hinges are made for round tube and pipe gates, like farm, ranch and livestock gates. They clamp or weld onto the pipe and turn on a pin or sleeve fixed to the post.

Honest note: nearly all pipe hinges sold today are zinc-plated steel, and we couldn't find a true stainless pipe hinge we'd recommend. If you want a rust-resistant hinge for a metal gate, use stainless weld-on barrel hinges instead; they work on round or flat frames. See our gate hinge guide for more options.

304 or 316 stainless?

For most indoor, inland and equipment use, 304 stainless is the right grade. Within about 5 miles of salt water, near pools, or around chemicals, choose 316. Either way, use matching stainless screws, and when welding, weld to stainless of the same grade or better. See 304 vs 316 for the full comparison.

Frequently asked questions

What is a flag hinge?

A flag hinge is a two-part lift-off hinge. One leaf has a fixed pin and the other has a hollow knuckle that drops over it, so you can lift the door off without removing a pin. They're used on equipment doors, gates, panels and metal doors that need to come off for access.

What's the difference between a single and double crank hinge?

A crank is a bend in the leaf. On a single (half) cranked hinge, one leaf is bent 90 degrees and the other stays flat. On a double (full) cranked hinge, both leaves are bent, which lets the door swing further open, often all the way back against the wall.

How do I know if I need a left- or right-hand flag hinge?

Flag hinges are handed, so they only work one way. A common rule: push the door open from the outside. If the hinges are on your right, you need right-hand hinges; if they're on your left, you need left-hand hinges. Check the seller's handing diagram before you order, since some brands define it differently.

What is a pivot hinge?

A pivot hinge carries a door from the top and bottom instead of along its edge. The door's weight goes into the floor and the head of the frame, which is why pivots suit heavy, oversized, double-acting and hidden doors. The two main types are center-hung and offset pivots.

What is a pipe hinge?

A pipe hinge is made for round tube or pipe gates, like farm and ranch gates. It clamps or welds onto the pipe and turns on a pin or sleeve on the post. Most are zinc-plated steel; for a rust-resistant option on a metal gate, use stainless weld-on barrel hinges instead.

Can stainless steel hinges be welded?

Yes. Weld-on stainless hinges are made for it. Weld them to stainless of the same grade or better, and use the right filler, so the weld area stays as rust-resistant as the hinge. Welding stainless to plain carbon steel creates a spot that can rust.
