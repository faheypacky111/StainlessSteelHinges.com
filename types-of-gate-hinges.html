Types of gate hinges

Updated November 2026 · independent guidance

The main types of gate hinges are strap, T-hinge, hook and strap, weld-on, self-closing and pivot hinges. Wood gates usually take strap or T-hinges, welded metal gates take weld-on hinges, and pool gates take self-closing hinges.

Strap hinges

Two long straps meet at a pin. The long leaves spread the gate's weight across more of the gate and post, so they resist sagging well. A good choice for wood gates and anywhere you want the classic look. Full comparison in strap hinge vs T-hinge.

T-hinges

One long strap on the gate and a short, wide leaf on the post, shaped like a T. The short leaf fits a narrow post face, which a strap hinge can't. Fine for light and medium gates; use a heavy-duty version for wider gates.

Hook and strap (screw hook) hinges

A threaded hook screws or bolts into the post and a strap on the gate drops over it. They hold heavy wood gates, and the gate lifts off for removal. Turning the hook in or out lets you adjust the gate a little.

Weld-on flag and barrel hinges

For welded metal gates. A barrel hinge is a pin inside a tube that you weld to the gate and post. A flag hinge has a flat plate (the “flag”) on one side, and many let the gate lift off. Match the hinge metal to the gate: stainless to stainless, steel to steel. Aluminum gates need aluminum or bolt-on hinges, since you can't weld steel to aluminum. See flag, pivot and pipe hinges and barrel hinges.

Self-closing gate hinges

A spring pulls the gate shut after you walk through. They're standard on pool gates, where local codes often require the gate to close and latch on its own. Check your local pool code before you buy. More in the pool gate hinges guide.

Pivot and heavy-duty bearing hinges

For heavy or wide gates, the weight needs to sit on a bearing instead of hanging off screws. Pivot hinges carry the gate on a pin at the top and bottom. Heavy-duty bearing hinges bolt through the post. Both swing smoothly under heavy loads. A gate that squeaks usually needs grease, not new hinges; see how to stop squeaky hinges.

Which type should you use?

Wood gate, average size

Strap or T-hinges. Use strap hinges if the gate is wide or heavy.

Heavy wood drive gate

Heavy hook and strap hinges, bolted through the post.

Steel or stainless gate

Weld-on flag or barrel hinges in the same metal as the gate.

Pool or child-safety gate

Self-closing hinges that meet your local pool code.

Near the coast or a pool? Use 316 stainless. Salt and chlorine rust lesser hinges fast. More in the outdoor hinges guide and the boat and dock hinges guide. Not sure? Try the gate hinge selector.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main types of gate hinges?

Strap hinges, T-hinges, hook and strap hinges, weld-on flag and barrel hinges, self-closing spring hinges, and pivot or heavy-duty bearing hinges.

What is the best hinge for a heavy gate?

Heavy-duty strap or hook and strap hinges bolted through the post for wood gates, and weld-on hinges for metal gates. Very heavy gates should sit on a bearing or pivot.

What hinges are best for a wooden gate?

Strap hinges for most wood gates. T-hinges if the post face is narrow. Use stainless steel or hot-dip galvanized hinges outdoors.

What hinge do I need for a metal gate?

For steel or stainless gates, weld-on barrel or flag hinges in the same metal as the gate. Many flag hinges let the gate lift off. Aluminum gates need aluminum or bolt-on hinges.

Do pool gates need self-closing hinges?

In many areas, yes. Pool codes often require gates to close and latch on their own. Check your local code.

Should gate hinges be 304 or 316 stainless?

304 is fine for most yards. Use 316 near saltwater or pools, since salt and chlorine attack 304.
